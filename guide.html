Getting set up

Install the app

Get it from Google Play and open it (the iPhone version is coming soon to the App Store). The app asks your height, weight, how active you are, and your goal: lose, maintain, or gain. From that it sets your daily calorie goal: about how many calories to eat each day to reach your aim.

Get your free AI key

The AI is what understands "turkey sandwich with mayo". You bring your own free key so the app can stay completely free. Right after setup the app walks you through it, one key at a time. Pick Groq (console.groq.com) or Gemini (aistudio.google.com); both are free, take about two minutes, and never ask for a credit card. The app walks you through every tap, then tests the key for you. Rather skip keys altogether? Premium runs on our AI instead.

Optional: the USDA key

Also free and instant (one short form, key arrives by email). It gives you your own lane into the US government food database instead of sharing the demo key with everyone. The app works without it.

Log your first meal

Tap "Add food". Type what you ate, or tap the mic and say it, or tap the camera and take a photo. Check the app's answer, fix anything that looks off, and log it. That is the whole job.

A day with AteRight

What using it actually feels like

Morning

Yesterday's breakfast is a one-tap button now. Tap, done, two seconds. The ring shows your whole day ahead of you, and the walk your watch recorded is already under it.

After class

Water aerobics ends at 10:45 and the phone asks "Water aerobics done?". Tap Done on the notification without opening the app. With exercise added to your goal, the ring grows a little.

Lunch out

Your usual place is already on the Menus tab from last time. Open it and the stars show what fits what is left today: "620 cal, fits, and packs 40g protein." New place? Photograph the menu once and it is saved for next time. Order, tap what you got, logged.

Snack attack

Scan the barcode on the package, say how many you had. "2 meat sticks" beats "68 grams" every time.

Dinner

Cooking from your recipe book: open the recipe, "Log a serving". New dish? The AI analyzes it once, with a health score, and it is in your book forever.

Evening glance

The ring says what is left, and today's dot fills in: logged at two meals, the day is complete. Went over? A calm note reminds you that one day never breaks anything, and the day still counts toward your Good Week. Saturday morning you weigh in, and every few weeks the app offers a small tune-up to your calorie goal based on your real trend.

Monday morning

If you turned on the Monday recap: "New week, fresh start. Last week: 6 of 7 days logged, your 7th Good Week in a row." With Premium, last week's report is waiting on the Trends tab.

Moving counts too

Logging exercise

Have a watch or fitness app?

Settings, Health Connect (Apple Health on iPhone). Turn on "Workouts and calories burned". Your workouts show on Today by themselves. Turn on the sharing switches too, and your watch and fitness apps see the meals you log here.

Keep track yourself?

On Today, tap "Say it" and talk: "45 minutes of water aerobics and walked the dog for 20". Your phone works it out; tap a row to change anything. Or tap "Add": activity, how long, Easy, Steady or Hard.

Do it regularly?

Make it a routine: the calendar icon on the Exercise card, or start from a ready-made one like chair exercises or a beginner walking plan. Pick the days and a time, and it shows on Today with a Done button.

Add it to your calorie goal?

The first time you log exercise the app asks: none, half or all. Half is our pick, because exercise estimates run high. Change it anytime in Settings, Exercise.

Good to know

Common questions

Is it really free? What is the catch?

Really free. No ads, no selling your data (your data never leaves your phone, so there is nothing to sell), and logging, menus, recipes, goals, diabetes support, Good Weeks and milestones are all free. The one piece of honesty: the free app runs on a free AI key you create yourself with Google or Groq. Their free tiers are generous and the app uses tiny requests, but those tiers belong to them, not us.

Then what is Premium?

An optional subscription ($5.99 a month or $39.99 a year) for people who would rather not set up a key: the AI runs on our service. It also adds reports (weekly, monthly, your meals, eating out, moving this week, carbs and blood sugar, and a summary for your care team), the Ask AteRight chat, weekly insights, and recipe photo scanning. Full comparison.

What is a Good Week?

Any week, Monday to Sunday, where you logged food on at least 5 days. Two days off never cost you anything, and a day over your calorie goal counts exactly like any other. Your progress screen shows your Good Weeks and milestones, and milestones are never taken away, even after a break.

Will the app nag me?

No. There are two reminders and both are off until you turn them on in Settings: a Monday morning recap, and an evening nudge that only appears on a day with nothing logged. Both are made on your phone.

How accurate are the calories?

Honest and labeled. Everyday foods are checked against the US government's USDA database. Barcode scans and label photos use the printed numbers. Chain items use published menus. When the AI estimates, the food says "estimated" right on it, and photo portions are good guesses you can adjust in one tap. For weight goals, consistency matters more than perfection, and the weekly weigh-in loop corrects for any systematic drift.

Do I have to weigh my food?

No. Portions work in human counts: 1 chicken breast, 2 tacos, half a serving. If you ever do weigh something, a grams option is one tap away.

What phones does it work on?

Android 8 and newer. Steps, workouts and weigh-ins come through Health Connect, which is built into modern Android (and a free app from Google on older phones). The iPhone version, working with Apple Health, is coming soon to the App Store.

Does logging exercise use AI?

Almost never. Your phone's own dictation turns speech into text, and AteRight works out the activity, how long and how hard right on your phone, using standard exercise research tables. Only an activity name it does not recognize, like the name of your class, is looked up once, and then it is remembered. Names of people are left out.

Will my watch and my own logs count twice?

No. If your watch recorded a workout at the same time as something you logged, the watch wins and your log shows crossed out. Tap "Count it" if they really were two different things.

Can it help with my diabetes?

There is an optional Diabetes support mode: carbs shown everywhere, plain-word blood sugar flags on foods, blood sugar notes on recipes, and menu suggestions that favor gentler options. It is information, not medical advice, and it never replaces your care team.

What happens if my AI provider has an outage?

If you saved more than one key, the app automatically retries your request with another provider and tells you it did. If a provider retires a model, one tap of "Test key" in Settings moves you to a current one.

How do I back up my data?

Settings, "Export backup". It writes one file with everything, including saved menus, milestones, exercise and routines. Copy it somewhere safe now and then, because your data lives only on your phone, which is the point.

Something is broken, or I have an idea

In the app: Settings, "Report a bug" or "Suggest an improvement". Or use the support page from any computer. Every report and idea gets read.
