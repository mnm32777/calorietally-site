Why people stick with it

Logging that takes seconds, not willpower

Most people quit food tracking in the first week because logging is a chore. AteRight attacks the chore.

💬

Just say it

"Turkey sandwich with mayo and a small apple." Type it or tap the mic and talk. The AI splits it into foods, portions, and calories.

📸

Or take a photo

Your plate, a barcode, a nutrition label, even a restaurant menu. Four camera modes, all one tap away.

⚡

Repeats are instant

Foods you eat often become one-tap buttons. After two weeks, most of your logging takes two seconds flat.

🔢

Counts, not grams

"Did you eat 1 meat stick or 2?" The app talks portions the way people do. Grams are there if you want them.

New: progress that rewards showing up

Good Weeks, not broken streaks

Log 5 days in a week and it is a Good Week. Two days off never cost you anything, and a day over budget counts exactly like any other. Your progress screen shows Good Weeks in a row, days logged, and milestones you can never lose, from your first meal to 365 days. Come back after a break and you earn "Welcome back", because coming back is the part that matters.

  • Seven dots under the ring show your week at a glance.
  • Milestones are yours for good: 7, 30, 100 and 365 days, Good Weeks, and firsts like your first photo log or saved menu.
  • A little celebration when a week turns Good. One tap and it is gone.
  • Optional reminders: a Monday morning recap, and an evening nudge only on a day with nothing logged.

Honest numbers

Calories you can actually trust

Every food shows where its numbers came from. Generic foods are checked against the US government's lab-measured USDA database. Packaged foods use the printed label. Chain restaurant items use the chain's own published menu data, and you can even pull the current numbers live from the web. Anything estimated says "estimated", right on it.

🏛️

USDA grounded

Lab-measured government data for everyday foods, not crowd-sourced guesses that disagree with each other.

🏷️

Reads labels

Point the camera at a Nutrition Facts panel and the printed numbers are read exactly. No guessing.

💚

Diabetes support

Optional mode that puts carbs up front and flags foods as steady, moderate, or fast carbs, in plain words.
